Benefits of Using Life Clock

2024-08-04 — Life Clock Research

Life Clock is a simple yet impactful tool: by aligning your clock to the real sunrise time, it brings several important benefits for health, productivity, and quality of life. In this article we explore these benefits.

1. Alignment with the body's natural circadian rhythm

The human body has an internal biological clock known as the circadian rhythm. This clock is sensitive to sunlight and controls important hormones such as melatonin (sleep), cortisol (wakefulness), and serotonin (mood). When you live by an official clock that may differ from the real sunrise time by up to an hour, this rhythm is disrupted.

Life Clock brings you closer to the moment of sunrise: when Life Clock reads 9:00, the sun has actually risen. This means you can make your daily decisions based on a real natural phenomenon, rather than on a conventional average.

2. Better sleep quality

Research shows that people who do calming activities near sunset (instead of intense activities) sleep better. Life Clock clearly shows the time of sunset — for example, when Life Clock reads 18:00, sunset is near, even if the official clock still reads 20:30 (in summer).

This allows your body to naturally transition into a sleep-ready state, instead of being forced to comply with an official clock that diverges from the real time.

3. Higher daily productivity

A 2019 study showed that employees whose start time was aligned with sunrise were about 7% more productive. The reason is simple: the human body is more alert in the early morning hours (solar time). Life Clock helps you identify these "golden hours."

For example, if in summer the official sunrise is at 05:00, then at 05:00 official time Life Clock reads 9:00. You can do your most important tasks in 9:00 to 12:00 Life Clock (i.e., 05:00 to 08:00 official), when the body is at peak alertness.

4. Less "running late" stress

Many of us, when the official clock shows 23:00, feel that it is "late" and we should sleep, even if our body is not yet ready for sleep. This is because in summer (with DST), the official clock runs ahead of the real solar time.

Life Clock shows you the reality: "It's 20:00 in Life Clock, meaning only 2 hours have passed since sunset. There's still plenty of time before sleep." This reduction in psychological pressure can be especially beneficial for people who struggle with nighttime anxiety.

5. Help with fasting and religious practices

For Muslims, knowing the real sunrise and sunset times matters — these times define the Fajr adhan, the Maghrib adhan, and the fasting interval. Life Clock naturally surfaces these times in its display: 9:00 Life Clock = sunrise, 18:00 Life Clock ≈ sunset.

This means a fasting person can tell how much of the fast has elapsed just by glancing at Life Clock, without a separate calculation.

6. Awareness of the seasons

When you look at Life Clock daily, you subconsciously notice seasonal changes. In summer, Life Clock runs ahead of official time (because sunrise is earlier). In winter, Life Clock gets closer to official time. This natural awareness of the seasons helps you stay in sync with the natural rhythm of the year.

7. Simpler international scheduling

If you work with people across different countries, official time can be confusing. But if everyone uses Life Clock, you can tell at a glance: "In your city it's 14:00 Life Clock right now, meaning it's afternoon. That's a good time for a business call."

Summary

Life Clock is a simple change in how time is displayed, but it can have a deep impact on your quality of life. By aligning your clock to the real sunrise, it helps you:

  • Stay in sync with your natural biological rhythm
  • Sleep better
  • Increase daily productivity
  • Reduce the stress caused by "official time"
  • Manage religious practices and fasting more easily
  • Stay aligned with seasonal changes
Life Clock does not replace official time — official time is essential for social coordination. But as a "second view" of time, it can help you live more mindfully.
