Biological Clock and Circadian Rhythm: Why Sunlight Matters

2024-08-04 — Life Clock Research

The human body has an internal clock that controls hormones, body temperature, and the level of alertness over the course of 24 hours. This clock is known as the circadian rhythm — a Latin word meaning "about a day." In this article we take a scientific look at this phenomenon and see why Life Clock helps with it.

What is the biological clock?

The circadian clock is a set of biological processes that repeat in a 24-hour cycle. This clock is located in a part of the brain called the hypothalamus (specifically, the suprachiasmatic nucleus, or SCN). The SCN is directly connected to the retina and receives light information.

When sunlight reaches the eye, the SCN sends signals to the body that:

  • Suppress the sleep hormone (melatonin)
  • Release the wakefulness hormone (cortisol)
  • Raise body temperature
  • Increase mental alertness

Why is official time not enough?

The human body is not sensitive to the wall clock — it is sensitive to light. When the official clock says 07:00 in the morning but it is winter and still dark, your body is still in sleep mode. Conversely, in summer when the official clock shows 21:00 but the sun is still up in the sky, your body thinks it is daytime and falling asleep becomes difficult.

This problem is especially amplified in regions that observe Daylight Saving Time (DST). Studies show that on the days after the clock change (especially in spring, when clocks jump forward one hour), heart attacks, traffic accidents, and workplace errors increase — all because of disruption to the circadian rhythm.

Melatonin: the sleep hormone

Melatonin is a hormone secreted by the pineal gland in the brain. Its secretion begins with darkness and is suppressed by light. Melatonin levels usually rise around 21:00 to 22:00 solar time and peak during the night.

If you try to sleep at 23:00 official (in summer, when the sun is still up in the sky), melatonin has not yet been secreted, and falling asleep will be difficult. Life Clock clearly shows this problem: at that same moment, Life Clock might read 20:00, meaning "it's still evening."

Cortisol: the wakefulness hormone

Cortisol is a hormone that peaks in the morning, about half an hour after waking. It gives you energy and prepares you for activity. But if you wake before sunrise (when it is still dark), cortisol has not yet been released and you feel tired.

Life Clock tells you that "when Life Clock reads 9:00, sunrise has happened — this is a suitable time to be fully awake." This can help you align your daily schedule with the natural rhythm of cortisol.

Key studies

Wright et al. (2013)

In this study, participants lived outdoors for a week, without access to artificial light. The result: their circadian rhythms aligned with sunrise and sunset times, even if they had previously diverged from official time. This shows that the human body naturally syncs to solar time, not to the wall clock.

Roenneberg et al. (2003)

This study of 50,000 people in Europe showed that people in the western parts of a time zone are more prone to "social jetlag" — meaning their biological clock is not aligned with official time. This disruption was linked to obesity, depression, and reduced academic performance.

Czeisler (1999)

This study showed that in an environment without time cues, the average human circadian rhythm is 24.2 hours (not exactly 24). That means our body naturally falls behind by about 12 minutes per day, and sunlight is what realigns it to 24 hours.
