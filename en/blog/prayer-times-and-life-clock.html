Prayer Times and Life Clock: Overlap and Differences

2024-08-04 — Life Clock Research

In Islam, prayer times are defined based on the position of the sun in the sky — which gives them a natural connection to Life Clock. In this article we explore this overlap and show how Life Clock can help Muslims.

What are the prayer times?

In Islam, the five daily prayers are performed at specific times that depend on the position of the sun:

  • Fajr: when the dawn light (before sunrise) appears on the horizon. Scientifically, the sun is 18° below the horizon.
  • Sunrise: the moment the upper edge of the sun appears on the horizon.
  • Dhuhr: when the sun passes the highest point of its daily arc (apparent noon). In the Shia school, it is usually placed a few minutes later for precaution.
  • Asr: when the shadow of an object equals its height (plus the noon shadow).
  • Maghrib: at sunset.
  • Isha: when the redness of sunset disappears from the horizon. Scientifically, the sun is 17° below the horizon.

Connection to Life Clock

Life Clock is defined by the real sunrise — and real sunrise is one of the prayer times. This means:

  • In Life Clock, 9:00 always equals the moment of sunrise.
  • The other prayer times (Dhuhr, Asr, Maghrib, Isha) can be converted to Life Clock, and their offset from sunrise changes throughout the year.

For example, in Tehran in summer:

  • Sunrise (Life Clock 9:00) ≈ official 05:08
  • Dhuhr (Life Clock ≈ 15:00) ≈ official 12:08
  • Maghrib (Life Clock ≈ 22:00) ≈ official 20:03

Benefits of using Life Clock for prayer times

1. Natural awareness of time

When you look at Life Clock and see 22:00, you know that sunset is near — even if the official clock still reads 20:00. This means you can prepare for the Maghrib adhan without needing to calculate or consult a calendar.

2. Unified visual display

In the Life Clock widget we designed, prayer times are displayed alongside their Life Clock equivalents. This means you can see "today's Dhuhr adhan is at 15:00 Life Clock," which gives you a better feel for time.

3. Removing ambiguity while traveling

When you travel to a city in another time zone, your official time changes, but the real sunrise time is also different. Life Clock automatically accounts for this difference. For example, if you travel from Tehran to Mashhad, sunrise in Mashhad happens about 6 minutes earlier than in Tehran, and Life Clock takes that into account.

Differences between Shia and Sunni

In computing prayer times, there are some small differences between Islamic schools:

  • Asr: In the Shia school, the shadow must reach 1× the object's height. In the Hanafi school, 2× the height.
  • Isha: In Shia, the sun is 17° below the horizon. In Sunni, usually 18°.
  • Maghrib: In Shia, a few minutes after sunset (to ensure the sun has fully set). In Sunni, immediately at sunset.

In our implementation we use the Shia (Jafari) method, which is prevalent in Iran. This means:

  • Asr is calculated with a shadow of 1× the object's height.
  • Isha is calculated with the sun at 17° below the horizon.
  • Maghrib = the moment of sunset (with a precaution of 1 minute).

How is it calculated?

Computing prayer times requires knowing the geographic position (latitude and longitude) and the date. We use the NOAA Solar Position formula, which is a mathematical approximation of the sun's position. This formula is fully described at esrl.noaa.gov/gmd/grad/solcalc/.

The accuracy of this formula at mid-latitudes (such as Iran) is about ±2 minutes — sufficient for most prayer times, but for Fajr and Isha, which are computed at low angles (17°–18° below the horizon), the error can reach ±5 minutes.

For higher accuracy, APIs such as aladhan.com or praytimes.org can be used. However, these APIs require an internet connection and have daily request limits. In the Life Clock Android app, we use the offline NOAA formula as the primary source, and offer weekly sync with the API as an optimization.

Display in the widget

In the Life Clock widget (located on the home page of the site), prayer times are displayed along with their Life Clock equivalents. This means you see:

  • Fajr: official 04:08 / Life Clock 8:00
  • Sunrise: official 05:08 / Life Clock 9:00
  • Dhuhr: official 12:08 / Life Clock 15:00
  • Asr: official 15:30 / Life Clock 18:22
  • Maghrib: official 20:03 / Life Clock 22:55
  • Isha: official 21:30 / Life Clock 24:22

Interesting note: Asr in Life Clock is typically around 18:00 — that is, 9 hours after sunrise. This consistency simplifies the visual layout of the day.
