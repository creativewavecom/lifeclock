Solar Time vs Clock Time: Why Do They Differ?

2024-08-04 — Life Clock Research

When you look at a clock, there are two different "times" that are often confused: local solar time (based on the actual position of the sun in the sky) and official clock time (a social convention). In this article we examine the differences.

What is local solar time?

Local solar time is defined as follows: when the sun is exactly at the highest point in the sky (due south for the Northern Hemisphere), the local solar clock reads 12:00 noon. This moment is called apparent noon.

Before and after apparent noon, solar time is calculated as follows: every 15 degrees of Earth's rotation equals 1 hour. So when the sun has moved 7.5 degrees past the highest point, solar time is 12:30.

This time is independent of your exact geographic position — every city has its own local solar time.

What is official clock time?

Official time (zone time) is a convention created in the 19th century to coordinate trains and industry. In this system, the entire Earth is divided into 24 time zones, each covering 15 degrees of longitude (1 hour). All points within a time zone share a single official time.

Iran is in a single time zone (UTC+3:30). This means someone in western Iran (such as Kermanshah) and someone in eastern Iran (such as Zahedan) both share the same official time, even though their local solar times may differ by up to 40 minutes.

Equation of Time

Even at a fixed location, local solar time changes slightly throughout the year. This happens for two reasons:

  1. Earth's axial tilt (23.5 degrees): causes the sun to appear at different elevations in the sky in different seasons.
  2. Earth's elliptical orbit: Earth orbits the sun in an ellipse and its speed varies throughout the year. When Earth is closer to the sun (perihelion, in early January), it moves faster.

Together, these two factors cause apparent noon to differ from "mean noon" by up to ±16 minutes over the year. This phenomenon is called the Equation of Time and must be accounted for when computing sunrise and sunset times.

Time zone offset

Iran officially sits in the +3:30 time zone. But the mean solar time of Iran (centered on Tehran, longitude 51.4° east) is:

Tehran solar time = UTC + 51.4/15 = UTC + 3:25:36

So Tehran's solar time is about 4 minutes and 24 seconds behind the official zone time (which is 3:30). In Shiraz (longitude 52.6° east), the difference reaches 1 minute. In Tabriz (longitude 46.3° east), the difference reaches 28 minutes!

This means if Tehran's official clock reads 12:00 noon, the sun in Tehran is actually at its highest point around 12:04. But in Tabriz, 12:00 official means the sun is still 28 minutes away from its peak — i.e., Tabriz's solar time is 11:32.

Daylight Saving Time (DST)

To complicate matters further, many countries move the clock forward by one hour in summer (DST). Iran used to observe DST (from Farvardin to Shahrivar) but abolished it starting in 1401.

DST causes official time in summer to run one hour ahead of mean solar time. This means in summer, the sun rises at 05:00 official (instead of 06:00), and sets at 20:00 official (instead of 19:00).

This shift disrupts the body's circadian rhythm. Studies have shown that on the days after the spring clock change, heart attacks rise by 24%.
