What is Life Clock?

2024-08-04 — Life Clock Research

Life Clock is a personal timekeeping system in which the time 9:00 always corresponds to the actual sunrise moment in your city. In other words, Life Clock is a dynamic offset from official time that is recalculated every day based on the sunrise time.

This idea may seem simple at first, but it has interesting implications: in summer, when sunrise occurs earlier, Life Clock runs ahead of official time. In winter, when sunrise is later, Life Clock gets closer to official time. In both cases, the moment of sunrise is always 9:00 in Life Clock.

Precise Definition

For any city on any day, Life Clock is calculated as follows:

lifeClock = officialTime + (9 hours − local sunrise time)

For example, if today's sunrise in Tehran is at 04:57 official time:

  • Difference: 9:00 − 04:57 = 4 hours and 3 minutes
  • At 08:10 official time (i.e., 3 hours and 13 minutes after sunrise), Life Clock reads: 9:00 + 3:13 = 12:13

Why 9 o'clock?

The number 9 is not arbitrary. In many cultures, 9 AM is considered the symbolic start of the active part of the day — a time when real activity usually happens, not at sunrise. Life Clock zeroes out this gap: sunrise = the start of the working day in Life Clock.

In addition, 9 o'clock is offset from midnight, so in Life Clock, midnight is always 03:00 (under the sunrise-at-9 assumption), which feels more natural than the value 00:00.

Difference from Official Time

Official time in a time zone is a calculated average for that entire zone. This means if you are at the western or eastern edge of a time zone, your official time can be up to 30 minutes off from the true solar time. Official time also follows Daylight Saving Time (DST), which is a human choice rather than a natural phenomenon.

Instead of relying on time zones and DST, Life Clock is directly tied to your geographic location. If you travel from Tehran to Shiraz, each city's Life Clock will be different (because sunrise times differ), even though both are in the same time zone.

History of the Idea

The idea of a "natural clock" or "solar clock" is not new. In the past, many cultures used local solar time, and the clock we know today is a product of the 19th century and the railways — when a standard time per zone was needed to coordinate train schedules.

"Life Clock" as a modern concept is an attempt to return to natural timekeeping, but with a twist: rather than setting the clock to the local sun, it anchors it to a reference point (sunrise = 9) so that it remains usable for everyday life.

Applications

  • Alignment with the circadian rhythm: Important activities (waking, eating, exercising) can be scheduled by Life Clock, in harmony with the body's natural rhythm.
  • Travel across time zones: Life Clock tells you "if I were in city X, where in the natural day am I right now," regardless of official time.
  • Productivity: Many people report that working by "natural time" instead of "wall-clock time" improves their productivity and energy.

How is it calculated?

Calculating the real sunrise time requires knowing the geographic position (latitude and longitude) and the date. In this project we use the NOAA formula, which is a mathematical approximation of the sun's position with an accuracy of about ±2 minutes. This formula works entirely offline — no server or API needed.
